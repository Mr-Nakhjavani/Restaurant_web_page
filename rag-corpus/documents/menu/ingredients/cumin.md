# Cumin (كمون)

Cumin as used at Bayt Al Ghaf: what this ingredient is, its allergens and diet class, and every dish and preparation that uses it, with quantities.

## Cumin: description, allergens and diet

Cumin (Arabic: كمون) is used in the Bayt Al Ghaf kitchen. Cumin seed, whole and ground.

- Diet class: vegan
- Allergens: none of the 14 major allergens
- May contain traces of: celery and mustard
- Used in 30 dishes and 6 preparations

Ground spices are packed by a supplier that also handles mustard and celery.

## Dishes that use Cumin

- Shorbat Adas (AED 26) uses 1 g of cumin.
- Nakhi (AED 22) uses 2 g of cumin.

## Sauces and preparations that use Cumin

- Bezar Spice Blend uses 20 g of cumin per batch.
- Dakkus Tomato-Chilli Sauce uses 3 g of cumin per batch.
- Mehyawa uses 4 g of cumin per batch.
- Tahini Lemon Sauce uses 1 g of cumin per batch.
- Al Ghaf House Red uses 3 g of cumin per batch.
- Al Ghaf House Green uses 2 g of cumin per batch.

## Questions guests ask about Cumin

**Q: Do you use cumin?**

A: Yes. Bayt Al Ghaf uses cumin in Shorbat Adas, Nakhi, Bezar Spice Blend, Dakkus Tomato-Chilli Sauce, Mehyawa, Tahini Lemon Sauce, Al Ghaf House Red and Al Ghaf House Green.

**Q: Which dishes contain cumin?**

A: Shorbat Adas, Nakhi, Bayd w Tamat, Weekend Fuala Breakfast Spread, Sambousa Trio, Hummus with Spiced Lamb & Pine Nuts, Mehyawa Regag Bites, Lamb Thareed, Saloonat Dajaj, Saloonat Samak (Monday), Margooga Laham (Tuesday), Madrooba Dajaj (Wednesday), Lamb Machboos, Chicken Machboos, Muhammar with Fried Safi, Camel Machboos (Thursday), Hammour Mashwi, Rubyan Mashwi, Rish Ghanam (Lamb Chops), Camel Kofta, Camel Burger with Loomi Aioli, Vegetable Machboos, Bamia Saloona, Pumpkin & Chickpea Saloona, Majlis Sharing Platter, Friday Family Machboos Platter, Whole Lamb Ouzi, Office Machboos Lunch Box, Ramadan Iftar Set Menu (per person) and Eid Al Etihad Feast (National Day).

**Q: Is cumin an allergen?**

A: Cumin is not one of the 14 major allergens, but it may contain traces of celery and mustard.
