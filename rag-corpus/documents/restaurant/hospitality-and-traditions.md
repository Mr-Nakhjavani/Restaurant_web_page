# Emirati hospitality at Bayt Al Ghaf

The Emirati customs guests meet at Bayt Al Ghaf: gahwa and dates on arrival, how coffee is poured and refused, family-style sharing, the majlis, and iftar in Ramadan.

## Gahwa and dates on arrival at Bayt Al Ghaf

Every table at Bayt Al Ghaf is welcomed with gahwa (Emirati coffee) poured from the dallah (a brass coffee pot) into small cups called finjan, served with Khalas dates.

## How gahwa is poured and refused

Gahwa is poured from right to left around the table, a little at a time. To say you have had enough, gently shake the finjan from side to side when handing it back.

## Sharing food the Emirati way

Emirati meals are shared. Bayt Al Ghaf portions its platters and machboos generously, and family-style serving is always offered.

## The majlis

A majlis is the traditional Emirati sitting room where guests are received, with cushions and floor seating. Bayt Al Ghaf has two private majlis rooms, Majlis Al Sidr and Majlis Al Ghaf.

## Iftar at Bayt Al Ghaf during Ramadan

During Ramadan, iftar seating at Bayt Al Ghaf starts at 17:30. Dates and laban are served at the sunset call to prayer (maghrib) to break the fast, followed by the Iftar Set Menu or dishes from the menu.
