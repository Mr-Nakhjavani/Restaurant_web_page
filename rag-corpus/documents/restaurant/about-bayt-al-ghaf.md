# About Bayt Al Ghaf (بيت الغاف)

Who Bayt Al Ghaf is: an Emirati restaurant and majlis on Dubai Creek — its name, story, chef, cuisine, dining rooms, signature dishes and values.

## Bayt Al Ghaf in one paragraph

Bayt Al Ghaf is a contemporary Emirati restaurant and majlis in the Al Seef heritage quarter on the Bur Dubai side of Dubai Creek. The kitchen cooks the dishes Emirati families eat at home — machboos, harees, thareed, saloona and luqaimat — with careful sourcing, house-ground bezar and slow, unhurried cooking, and serves them in a dining room of coral-stone walls, palm-frond ceilings and creek views.

Bayt Al Ghaf (Arabic: بيت الغاف) describes itself as “Emirati kitchen & majlis on Dubai Creek”. It opened in 2019. Cuisine: Emirati, Gulf (Khaleeji) and Contemporary Emirati.

## What the name Bayt Al Ghaf means

Bayt Al Ghaf means “House of the Ghaf” — the ghaf (Prosopis cineraria) is the UAE's national tree, a symbol of shade, endurance and hospitality in the desert. The restaurant's sign is a brass ghaf tree on a coral-stone building.

## The story of Bayt Al Ghaf

Bayt Al Ghaf began with a family majlis in Al Bastakiya and a ghaf tree in the courtyard, where neighbours gathered for gahwa, dates and long conversations.

Chef Hessa grew up a few streets from the creek, in a house where machboos was cooked every Friday and harees simmered all night before Eid. Bayt Al Ghaf brings that kitchen to Al Seef: the same unhurried methods, the same respect for rice, lamb, dates and loomi, served in a coral-stone dining room that looks out over the abras.

## Chef Hessa, head chef of Bayt Al Ghaf

Chef Hessa is the head chef of Bayt Al Ghaf. She grew up in Al Bastakiya, a few streets from Dubai Creek and a few minutes' walk from the restaurant. Her family cooked machboos every Friday and harees overnight before Eid, and the Bayt Al Ghaf menu is built on those home recipes, alongside a few contemporary dishes that use local camel, dates and loomi.

## How the Bayt Al Ghaf kitchen cooks

- The bezar spice blend is toasted and ground in-house every week.
- Regag bread is stretched on the hot iron plate in front of guests, and chebab is baked to order.
- Seafood comes from licensed Gulf suppliers; hammour is bought only outside the seasonal fishing ban.
- Harees is cooked overnight in one batch, so it is limited each day.
- All meat and poultry are halal, and the restaurant is alcohol-free.
- Contemporary dishes include camel kofta with the walnut-and-pepper Al Ghaf House Red sauce, a camel burger glazed with Sauce No. 7, and ice cream churned from UAE camel milk and dates.

## Bayt Al Ghaf dining rooms and seats

Bayt Al Ghaf has 154 seats: 88 indoors (the main dining hall and the creek-view window tables), 36 on the seasonal creek terrace and 30 in two private majlis rooms with traditional floor seating (Majlis Al Sidr and Majlis Al Ghaf). The dining room has coral-stone walls, palm-frond ceilings and views over the abras and dhows on the creek.

## Bayt Al Ghaf signature dishes

- Balaleet (بلاليط), AED 34: Sweet saffron and cardamom vermicelli crowned with a thin omelette.
- Harees (هريس), AED 48: Wheat and lamb slow-cooked overnight and beaten smooth, finished with ghee.
- Lamb Thareed (ثريد لحم), AED 62: Lamb and vegetable saloona ladled over layers of regag bread.
- Lamb Machboos (مجبوس لحم), AED 72: Our signature: bone-in lamb shoulder and spiced basmati with loomi and hashu.
- Muhammar with Fried Safi (محمر وصافي), AED 69: Sweet saffron and date-syrup rice with a whole fried rabbitfish.
- Luqaimat (لقيمات), AED 32: Crisp saffron-cardamom dumplings, warm dibs and toasted sesame.
- Gahwa Emirati (Dallah for the Table) (قهوة عربية), AED 30: A dallah of lightly roasted Arabic coffee with cardamom and saffron, served with dates.
- Karak Chai (شاي كرك), AED 12: Strong black tea simmered with evaporated milk, cardamom and saffron.
- Friday Family Machboos Platter (صينية مجبوس الجمعة), AED 349: Fridays only. Serves 5: lamb shoulder and whole chicken machboos on one great tray.
- Whole Lamb Ouzi (أوزي), AED 1,450: Catering only. Serves 10–12: whole slow-roasted lamb on spiced rice with nuts.

## Bayt Al Ghaf values: welcome, generosity and respect

- Welcome (الترحيب): every table is welcomed with gahwa from the dallah and Khalas dates. Coffee is poured from right to left; a gentle shake of the finjan says you have had enough.
- Generosity (الكرم): Emirati meals are shared. Platters and machboos are portioned generously, and family-style serving is always offered.
- Respect (الاحترام): a family restaurant — alcohol-free, smoke-free and modest. Children are welcome, and the majlis rooms give families privacy.
